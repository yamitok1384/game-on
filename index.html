Partners in crime

The police catch two friends, A and B. They are in different rooms and they can't talk.

Each friend has two options: stay silent or confess (tell the police that the other friend did it). The years in prison depend on what both friends decide.

stay silent = callar · confess = confesar / delatar · prison = cárcel · years = años

Two partners in crime, A and B, have been arrested and are being questioned in separate rooms, so they cannot agree on a plan.

The detective offers each of them the same deal: stay loyal and keep quiet, or confess and blame your partner to get a shorter sentence. The final result depends on both decisions. Will you trust your partner, or protect yourself?

Why? The maths behind the game

Mediation challenge: read the explanation in English with your partner. Then one of you explains it to the class in Spanish, in your own words. Finally, check your explanation with the Spanish version.
  1. Look at the game from Player A's point of view. A doesn't know what B will do, so A thinks about both possibilities.
  2. If B stays silent: A gets 1 year if A stays silent, but 0 years if A confesses. Confessing is better for A.
  3. If B confesses: A gets 10 years if A stays silent, but 5 years if A confesses. Confessing is better for A again.
  4. So, whatever B does, confessing is better for A. B thinks exactly the same way.
  5. The surprise: if both of them confess, they get 5 years each. If both of them had stayed silent, they would only get 1 year each. Thinking only about yourself makes both of you lose. That is the dilemma.

With probabilities (the computer challenge):

  1. Miramos el juego desde el punto de vista del jugador A. A no sabe qué hará B, así que piensa en las dos posibilidades.
  2. Si B se calla: A tiene 1 año si se calla, pero 0 años si confiesa. Al jugador A le conviene confesar.
  3. Si B confiesa: A tiene 10 años si se calla, pero 5 años si confiesa. Al jugador A le vuelve a convenir confesar.
  4. Haga lo que haga B, a A le conviene confesar. Y B piensa exactamente igual.
  5. La sorpresa: si los dos confiesan, tienen 5 años cada uno. Si los dos se hubieran callado, tendrían solo 1 año cada uno. Pensar solo en uno mismo hace que pierdan los dos. Ese es el dilema.

Sofia's boxes

Sofia is on a TV game show. There are three boxes. Only one box has €10,000 inside. The other two are empty.

Sofia chooses a box. The presenter knows where the money is. He opens one of the other boxes, and it is always empty: he never opens Sofia's box or the prize box.

Then he asks: "Do you want to keep your box or switch?"

empty = vacía · knows = sabe · never = nunca · keep = quedarse con · switch = cambiar

Sofia, a 16-year-old student, is taking part in a TV game show. In front of her there are three identical boxes, but only one of them contains a €10,000 prize.

After Sofia picks a box, the presenter, who knows exactly where the prize is, opens one of the other two boxes. It is empty, of course: he would never reveal the prize. Now only two boxes remain closed, and Sofia must decide: does she keep her first choice or switch to the other box? Is it really a 50/50 decision?

Why? The maths behind the game

Mediation challenge: read the explanation in English with your partner. Then one of you explains it to the class in Spanish, in your own words. Finally, check your explanation with the Spanish version.
  1. When Sofia chooses her first box, she has only a 1 in 3 chance of choosing the prize. So there is a 2 in 3 chance that the prize is in one of the other two boxes.
  2. The presenter knows where the prize is and always opens an empty box. This doesn't change Sofia's first chance: it is still 1 in 3.
  3. If her first box has the prize (1 in 3 times), switching makes her lose.
  4. If her first box is empty (2 in 3 times), the presenter has to leave the prize box closed. Switching makes her win.
  5. So switching wins 2 out of 3 times, and keeping wins only 1 out of 3. It is not 50/50!
  1. Cuando Sofía elige su primera caja, solo tiene 1 posibilidad entre 3 de acertar. Por tanto, hay 2 posibilidades entre 3 de que el premio esté en una de las otras dos cajas.
  2. El presentador sabe dónde está el premio y siempre abre una caja vacía. Eso no cambia la primera probabilidad de Sofía: sigue siendo 1 entre 3.
  3. Si su primera caja tenía el premio (1 de cada 3 veces), al cambiar pierde.
  4. Si su primera caja estaba vacía (2 de cada 3 veces), el presentador está obligado a dejar cerrada la caja del premio. Al cambiar, gana.
  5. Así que cambiar gana 2 de cada 3 veces y quedarse gana solo 1 de cada 3. ¡No es 50/50!

Bonus: imagine 52 boxes

Still not sure? Imagine 52 boxes, like a deck of cards. Sofia picks one: she has only a 1 in 52 chance. The presenter opens 50 empty boxes and leaves only one other box closed. Would you keep your box or switch? Try it once!

With 52 boxes, keeping wins only 1 out of 52 times and switching wins 51 out of 52 times. It's the same idea as with 3 boxes: keeping wins 1 out of 3, switching wins 2 out of 3. / Con 52 cajas, quedarse gana solo 1 de cada 52 veces y cambiar gana 51 de cada 52. Es la misma idea que con 3 cajas.
